* Introduce yourself. What kind of work do you do? When did you start voice training? Who are your voice models?
* Tell your weekend plans. How did you decide? How long have you been planning this?
* Speak for 90 seconds about your interests and hobbies. What got you into this? What's your next investment of time or money?
* What single thing would you most like to change about the voice-training community at large?

* How did you finally come to the conclusion that VFS was right for you? And how did you decide on which version of the procedure you would pursue?
* What was the spark that made you realize that, even after VFS, you'd still need to use what you learned from voice training?

* Where were you, and what were you doing, when you had your biggest breakthrough? What plateau were you stuck at, and how long had you been stuck?

* Did you ever worry that you would not succeed? What kept hope alive for you?

* When you were new at this, what did you most misunderstand about voice training?

* Do you help others with voice?
* What do you see as the most common errors and challenges around the self-taught approach?
* Have you ever paid for lessons? If so, how did you decide who to sign up with? If no, what drove your decision?

* How did you divide your time between self-study, exploration, and working with coaches both amateur and professional?
* What are you currently working on in your voice?
* Without naming names, what is the worst "tip" or advice you've ever gotten about your voice? What made it so bad?
* Have you ever had vocal damage, pain with speaking, or anything like that? If so, how do you suppose it happened and how did you fix it?
* Considering all the people you've worked with or taken advice from, and again without naming names, what was the best aspect of someone's teaching strategy or style?

* In 25 words or less, what advice would you offer to new voice learners?

* Thank you so much for the privilege of speaking with you today!
