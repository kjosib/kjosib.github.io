# On Passing

There are cultural aspects to "passing", and there are dangers to passing judgement on the gender of someone's voice. The point is not hug-boxing; it's the recognition that standards and social pressures differ across space and time, as do people's responses to them. Voices are (quite clearly) extremely malleable, but societies fail to recognize this. Accordingly, speech communities apply subtle and sometimes overt pressure for people to conform to local stereotypes: Both men and women *tend,* most of the time, to imitate those around them who they want to be accepted and respected by. If that means holding a different vocal posture *without necessarily being aware of the fact,* then over time it will slowly creep into someone's default behavior. That's why, for example, there are a decent number of trans German women who very clearly get all the social signals of passing *locally* (e.g. being frau(line) on the telephone) but my General American ears want to hear a higher larynx (smaller sound) to be quite convinced. Meanwhile some of us girls probably sound positively squeaky in comparison.

Passing is not something you do. It's something done to you.
